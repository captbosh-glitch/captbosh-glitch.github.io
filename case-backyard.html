Five screens, then a hard critique

The look was right; the safety story wasn’t

The problem

The first Figma pass looked like an old field guide, but reviewing it against the safety rules showed where the design quietly broke them. “What did you spot?” invited the exact guess the product had ruled out.

What I did

I designed the core flow in Figma, then worked through the critique: the camera became “Compare a photo,” harm levels got written labels and a meter you can read without color, and every count now comes from real research-grade sightings within 10 miles.

What changed

Eight decisions that made it trustworthy, from the camera to the harm scale.

02

Built for the trail

Dogs, emergencies and no signal

The problem

The idea began with a dog, but no screen considered dogs. And a trail app gets used with one bar of signal, or none.

What I did

A dog switch re-rates every hazard and adds dog-specific advice. A red Emergency button is on every guide screen. “Save for offline” keeps a place’s guide on the phone, and there’s no account to sign into at the trailhead.

What changed

The right action is one tap away, with or without a connection.
