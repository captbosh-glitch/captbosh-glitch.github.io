01

Intranet and app research

Testing a new intranet and mobile app with real employees before launch

The problem

The company was about to launch a new intranet and companion mobile app. Leadership wanted it tested with real users first, to find friction while it could still be fixed.

What I did

I led the research: surveys, one-on-one interviews and panel interviews. At a company communications conference I ran a live usability session with more than 50 people, then turned what we heard into prioritized requirements for the development team.

What changed

The pilot changed the plan. The app was built around news, but people used it for quick tasks like approvals, desk booking and expenses. The team’s three priorities became: remove friction, bring in core apps like Workday, and design for getting tasks done.

02

Data Marketplace

One place to find, request and use the company’s data

The problem

Finding and getting access to company data meant a different path every time, with barriers around security, tool access and technical skill.

What I did

With the product team, I mapped the gaps in the current process and defined two main users: the data consumer and the data product owner. I co-created wireframes in Miro to agree on a North Star vision, mapped every screen path, then built high-fidelity prototypes in Adobe XD.

What changed

A tested framework for development, built around the two people who actually use it.

03

Quarterly report building

Taking the manual work out of a quarterly report

The problem

To build each quarterly report, analysts gathered data by hand from several sources, made charts, pasted them into PowerPoint, wrote commentary, then went through rounds of review.

What I did

I moved the data into an AWS data lake so analysts could connect directly, then ran an A/B test across two business units: one used Power BI with PowerPoint, the other Power BI with Excel. People wanted Excel for analysis and PowerPoint for presenting, so I designed a hybrid: Power BI models, Excel charts, live-linked into PowerPoint.

What changed

No more hand-gathered data, fewer errors, and 8 to 15 hours saved per analyst every report cycle.

04

Forecast dashboards in Power BI

Live pipeline status and program health, designed and built

The problem

The forecast team’s reports depended on about 19 data pipelines across several systems, and nobody could see whether last night’s runs had worked. Their modernization program was tracked informally across many owners.

What I did

I was the product designer on a four-person team. For each dashboard I wireframed in Miro AI, refined it with stakeholders, then built it in Power BI: a card per pipeline with pass/fail, timing and a 30-day history; and a KPI view with red/amber/green status, named owners and quarter-over-quarter trends.

What changed

Both are in production. The team checks one live view instead of running manual checks, and leadership has one source of truth that already surfaced a workstream running well behind its target.

05

Statutory reporting dashboard

Seeing when the data will land, before a legal deadline

The problem

Regulatory reports have strict legal deadlines, but analysts couldn’t see when the data they needed would arrive or whether it was complete.

What I did

I researched how analysts worked against their deadlines, iterated through wireframes with stakeholders on the charts and status levels, and built interactive prototypes in Adobe XD for engineering.

What changed

A dashboard that tracks data arrival and completeness, so analysts can plan instead of guess.
