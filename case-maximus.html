01

HealthChoice Illinois

Choosing a health plan and finding a doctor, for the State of Illinois

The problem

Enrollment is something people do once a year and then forget. Illinois needed a site where anyone could pick a health plan and find a provider without calling for help.

What I did

I led a team of UX designers and researchers. We combined call-center feedback with web analytics to find where people got stuck, then I led the wireframes and high-fidelity Adobe XD prototypes that went to engineering for production.

What changed

More applications were completed online and call-center volume went down. The site won the Web Marketing Association’s 2022 Government Standard of Excellence and Health Care Standard of Excellence awards.

Healthy Louisiana

Health plans, dental plans and providers, on the web and in an app

The problem

Louisiana’s Medicaid members had to choose health plans, dental plans and providers. Many of them didn’t have a computer at home.

What I did

With two UX designers, product and a large engineering team, I studied call-center feedback and analytics to find friction in the old process. I led the wireframes and prototypes, handed responsive mockups to web engineering, and built asset packages for the iOS and Android teams.

What changed

Online completions rose and calls went down. The mobile app reached members with no home computer, so they could enroll from their phones.

03

Who are our Medicaid customers?

A persona system any team could keep up to date

The problem

To build a design culture around real people, the teams needed a shared answer to one question: who are our Medicaid customers?

What I did

Working with UX researchers, I turned the findings into detailed personas: background, Medicaid literacy, enrollment goals, and an emotional journey I called “process moods.” I built them as editable templates so any team could update them or add new ones.

What changed

Product, design and engineering worked from the same picture of the people they served, and the templates kept it current as enrollment changed.
